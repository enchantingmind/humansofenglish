IELTS preparation

Build the skills for your target IELTS score.

IELTS assesses listening, reading, writing and speaking. There are two versions: Academic, usually for university study, and General Training, often for work or migration. Listening and Speaking are the same in both.

We help you understand what each band score means in practice, then focus your time on the skills that will move your score.

IELTS at a glance
SectionTimeWhat it involves
ListeningAbout 30 minFour recorded parts with 40 questions, from everyday conversations to an academic talk.
Reading60 minThree sections with 40 questions. Academic uses longer, more academic texts; General Training uses everyday and workplace texts.
Writing60 minTask 1: describe visual information (Academic) or write a letter (General Training). Task 2: an essay in both versions.
Speaking11–14 minA three-part conversation with an examiner: familiar topics, a long turn, then a discussion.

What we practise for IELTS

  • Reading the band descriptors so you know what examiners reward
  • Planning and writing Task 1 and Task 2 within the time limit
  • All three Speaking parts, with feedback on fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation
  • Skimming, scanning and managing time across Reading passages
  • Listening for detail, following signposts and spelling answers correctly

This summary is for orientation. Test formats can change, so always confirm details on the official IELTS website before you book.

TOEFL preparation

Practise academic English for every TOEFL section.

TOEFL iBT is a computer-based test of academic English, taken at a test centre or at home. Your speaking is recorded rather than spoken to a live examiner, so a different kind of preparation helps.

We build your academic vocabulary, note-taking and timed responses, so you can stay calm and organised on screen.

TOEFL iBT sections
SectionWhat it involves
ReadingUnderstanding academic and everyday texts, including vocabulary in context and the writer's main ideas.
ListeningFollowing lectures, conversations and announcements, often while taking notes.
WritingWritten responses on screen, including taking part in an academic discussion.
SpeakingShort recorded spoken responses to prompts, with limited preparation time.

What we practise for TOEFL

  • Academic vocabulary and the language of lectures and seminars
  • Note-taking that helps you answer, not just record
  • Clear spoken answers within short time limits, without memorised scripts
  • Organised written responses and comfortable typing speed

ETS updates the TOEFL iBT from time to time, including timings, task types and how scores are reported. Check the official TOEFL website for the current format before you book.

Other exams

Preparing for a different English test?

Many universities, employers and immigration routes accept other tests too. The skills underneath are the same, so we can help you build them, while you check which test your destination accepts.

Cambridge English

B2 First, C1 Advanced and more

Level-based exams covering reading, writing, listening, speaking and use of English, with a paired speaking test.

Pearson

PTE Academic

A computer-based test where speaking and writing are assessed together through integrated tasks.

Duolingo

Duolingo English Test

An online, adaptive test taken at home, with short tasks that change in difficulty as you answer.

Test names belong to their owners. Humans of English is independent and is not affiliated with any test provider.

Our method

How we prepare you

Exam preparation works best when it builds real English, not just test tricks. Here is the path we follow with every exam learner.

Plan your preparation

  1. Start with your target

    Which test, which score, and by when. Everything else is planned backwards from that.

  2. Find your gaps

    A practice task in each skill shows where you are strong and where marks are being lost.

  3. Learn the format

    Question types, timing and what examiners look for, so nothing on the day is a surprise.

  4. Practise with feedback

    Regular timed practice, with specific feedback on your speaking and writing.

  5. Rehearse test day

    Full practice under exam conditions, plus a plan for timing and nerves.
