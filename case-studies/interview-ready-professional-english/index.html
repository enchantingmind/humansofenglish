Getting interview-ready in professional English

Experienced in their field but unsure how to present it in English. A six-week plan for introductions, interview answers and follow-up emails.

  • Professional English
  • Interviews
  • Presentation
  • 6-week plan

This is an illustrative example, not a real student's story. It shows how a learning plan for this kind of goal could be structured. Every learner's plan is built around their own starting point.

At a glance

  • Starting pointExperienced in their field and comfortable with technical vocabulary. Finds it hard to talk about achievements clearly and concisely.
  • GoalFeel ready for interviews in English for a new role.
  • Time availableFour to five hours a week for six weeks.
  • Main focusIntroductions, structured answers, and professional email.

The starting point

This learner uses English at work every day, mostly in writing and technical discussions. In interview-style practice, though, their answers are long and unfocused. They describe what "the team" did, undersell their own role, and struggle when a question is unexpected.

The plan focuses on structure and clarity: saying more with less, and sounding confident about real experience.

The six-week plan

  1. Week 1The one-minute introductionBuild a present → past → future introduction. Record it, cut it down, and record it again.
  2. Week 2STAR storiesTurn real work experiences into three or four STAR answers that can be adapted to different questions. Practise using "I" for your own actions.
  3. Week 3Handling the unexpectedPhrases for buying time and asking for clarification. Practise with unfamiliar questions.
  4. Week 4Strengths, weaknesses and motivationLanguage for talking about yourself positively but honestly. Prepare thoughtful questions for the interviewer.
  5. Week 5Mock interviewsTwo full practice interviews with detailed feedback on content, clarity, pace and pronunciation.
  6. Week 6Follow-up and polishWrite a short thank-you email after an interview. Final mock interview and a personal checklist for the day.

How progress is checked

  • Answer length and structure: does each answer have a clear point and finish on a result?
  • Comparing week 1 and week 5 interview recordings.
  • The learner's own confidence rating before and after each mock interview.

What this example shows

  • Strong general English doesn't automatically mean strong interview English.
  • Structure makes experienced professionals sound clearer and more confident.
  • Using your own real work as practice material makes preparation more useful.
