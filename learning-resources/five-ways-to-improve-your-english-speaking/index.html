Five Ways to Improve Your English Speaking

Simple daily habits that build fluency, from shadowing to speaking notes, without needing a partner every day.

6 min read·All levels·Humans of English team

Many learners say the same thing: "I understand a lot, but when I try to speak, the words disappear." That gap between what you know and what you can say is normal. The good news is that speaking is a skill like any other, and it improves with the right kind of practice, not just more of it.

Here are five habits you can start today. None of them needs a teacher or a partner every day, but all of them work even better with feedback.

1. Speak a little every day

Ten minutes of speaking every day is worth more than one long session a week. Your mouth, your memory and your confidence all need regular use. If you have no one to talk to, talk to yourself: describe what you're doing, plan your day out loud, or explain something you read.

Example "Okay, first I'm going to make some coffee. Then I need to reply to two emails. After that I'll probably go for a walk, because the weather looks nice."

2. Learn phrases, not just words

Fluent speakers don't build every sentence word by word. They use ready-made chunks: "to be honest", "it depends on…", "the thing is…". When you learn a new word, learn it inside a short phrase you could actually say.

Single worddecision
Useful chunk"I need to make a decision by Friday."

3. Try shadowing

Shadowing means listening to a short recording and speaking along with it, copying the rhythm, stress and intonation as closely as you can. It trains your mouth to produce natural English and your ear to notice how words connect.

  1. Choose a clip of 15–30 seconds with a transcript.
  2. Listen twice while reading the transcript.
  3. Play it again and speak at the same time as the speaker.
  4. Repeat until it feels smooth, then try without the transcript.

4. Record yourself and listen back

Recording yourself can feel uncomfortable at first, but it's one of the fastest ways to improve. You'll notice repeated words, long pauses and pronunciation habits that you can't hear while you're speaking.

Try answering the same question once a week, for example "What do you enjoy doing at the weekend?", and keep the recordings. After a month, listen to the first and the last. Hearing your own progress is a powerful motivator.

5. Use speaking notes, not scripts

Writing a full script and memorising it often makes speaking harder, because when you forget one word, the whole thing collapses. Instead, write three or four keywords and speak from them.

Speaking notes: "Describe your hometown" coast · small · fish market · quiet in winter

Keywords keep you organised but leave you free to find the words in the moment, which is exactly what real conversation asks you to do.

Your one-week challenge

  • Talk to yourself for five minutes every day.
  • Learn three new useful chunks and use each one out loud.
  • Shadow one short clip, three times.
  • Record a one-minute answer on Monday and again on Sunday.
