At a glance

  • Starting pointGood understanding of grammar and reading. Rarely speaks, and translates in their head before answering.
  • GoalHold relaxed everyday conversations with neighbours, colleagues and new friends.
  • Time availableAbout 20 minutes a day, plus one guided session a week.
  • Main focusFluency, everyday phrases and listening to natural speech.

The starting point

This is a very common profile among English learners: someone who studied English at school, knows a lot of grammar, and understands much of what they read, but freezes when someone speaks to them. The problem isn't knowledge. It's that this knowledge has never been used under real-time pressure.

So the plan doesn't add more grammar. It turns what the learner already knows into speech, through frequent, low-pressure practice.

The eight-week plan

  1. Weeks 1–2Break the silenceTalk to yourself daily: describe your day, plan out loud. Learn ten conversation "survival" phrases, like asking for repetition and buying time.
  2. Weeks 3–4Chunks, not wordsLearn phrases for small talk, reacting and asking follow-up questions. Start shadowing 20-second clips from everyday conversations.
  3. Weeks 5–6Real conversationsWeekly guided conversations on everyday topics. Practise telling short stories about your week, with linking words like "then", "after that" and "in the end".
  4. Weeks 7–8Out in the worldSet one real-life speaking challenge per week, such as asking a question in a shop or chatting with a colleague. Reflect on what went well and what to practise next.

A typical day

ActivityTime
Shadow a short clip5 min
Review three phrases and use each one out loud5 min
Talk to yourself about your day, or record a one-minute answer10 min

How progress is checked

  • A one-minute recording on the same question every week, compared over time.
  • Noticing how often the learner translates in their head, and how long pauses last.
  • A simple log of real-life conversations and how they felt.

What this example shows

  • Many "quiet" learners need practice, not more rules.
  • Short daily speaking beats long, occasional study sessions.
  • Confidence comes from small real-world wins, so plan for them deliberately.
