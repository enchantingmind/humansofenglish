In IELTS Writing Task 2 you write an essay of at least 250 words in response to a question about an issue or opinion. It is the same task in both Academic and General Training. It's worth more than Task 1, so it deserves about 40 of your 60 minutes.

A clear structure won't write the essay for you, but it frees your mind to focus on ideas and language. Here is a simple, flexible approach.

Step 1: Understand the question (2–3 minutes)

Most Task 2 questions ask you to do one of these:

  • Give your opinion: "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
  • Discuss both views: "Discuss both views and give your own opinion."
  • Problems and solutions, or causes and effects.
  • Advantages and disadvantages: sometimes "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"

Underline every part of the question. A common reason for lower scores is answering only part of what was asked.

Step 2: Plan (5 minutes)

Decide your position, then note one main idea for each body paragraph, with a reason and an example. Five minutes of planning saves far more time later.

Step 3: Write in four paragraphs

ParagraphPurposeApprox. length
IntroductionParaphrase the topic and state your position clearly.2–3 sentences
Body 1Main idea → explanation → example.4–6 sentences
Body 2Second main idea, or the other view, developed the same way.4–6 sentences
ConclusionSummarise your position. No new ideas.2 sentences

An annotated example

Question: Some people believe that university students should study whatever they like. Others think they should only study subjects that will be useful in the future, such as science and technology. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
Introduction: paraphrase + position "There is ongoing debate about whether university students should be free to choose any subject or should focus on fields with clear career value, such as science and technology. While both views have merit, I believe students should be free to choose, provided they understand the practical implications of their choice."
Body 1: the first view, developed "On the one hand, prioritising science and technology may benefit both students and society. [main idea] These fields are linked to growing industries, so graduates may find work more easily. [explanation] For example, many economies are investing heavily in renewable energy and digital services, creating demand for engineers and data specialists. [example]"
Body 2: the other view, plus your opinion "On the other hand, students who study a subject they are passionate about are often more motivated and successful. [main idea] Moreover, societies need historians, artists and writers as well as scientists… [development continues]"
Conclusion: summary only "In conclusion, although focusing on useful subjects has clear economic advantages, I believe students should choose freely, as motivation and a range of skills benefit both individuals and society."

Step 4: Check (3–5 minutes)

Leave a few minutes to read your essay. Look for your personal common mistakes: articles, verb tenses, plurals or spelling. Check that every part of the question is answered and that your opinion is consistent from the introduction to the conclusion.

Task 2 timing checklist

  • 2–3 minutes: understand the question
  • 5 minutes: plan your position and main ideas
  • About 30 minutes: write four paragraphs
  • 3–5 minutes: check and correct
  • Aim for around 260–300 words. Quality matters more than length.
