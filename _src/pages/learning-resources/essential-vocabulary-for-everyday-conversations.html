Everyday conversations follow patterns. People greet each other, make small talk, ask for things, react and say goodbye in fairly predictable ways. Learning the phrases for each stage gives you a toolkit you'll use every single day.

Don't try to learn them all at once. Choose two or three from each group, practise them out loud, and use them in your next conversation.

Starting a conversation

  • "Hi, how's it going?"Friendly and informal, for colleagues and friends.
  • "Nice to meet you. I'm…"When meeting someone for the first time.
  • "How was your weekend?"A simple, safe opener on a Monday.
  • "Sorry to bother you, but…"Politely starting a conversation with a stranger.

Keeping the conversation going

Short reactions show you're listening and encourage the other person to continue. Follow-up questions keep things moving.

  • "Oh really? What happened?"Shows interest and invites more detail.
  • "That sounds great / awful!"Reacting to news, good or bad.
  • "How about you?"Turning the question back after you answer.
  • "What do you mean by…?"Asking for clarification without stopping the conversation.

Asking for help or repetition

Asking someone to repeat is not a weakness. Fluent speakers do it all the time.

  • "Sorry, could you say that again?"The most useful phrase for any learner.
  • "Could you speak a bit more slowly, please?"Polite and completely normal to ask.
  • "How do you say … in English?"When you know the idea but not the word.
  • "Could you give me a hand with this?"Informal way to ask for help.

Agreeing and disagreeing politely

  • "Exactly. / I know what you mean."Agreeing naturally.
  • "I see your point, but…"Disagreeing gently.
  • "I'm not so sure about that."Softer than "I disagree".
  • "Maybe, although…"Adding a different view without conflict.

Ending a conversation

Ending a conversation politely is a skill in itself. English speakers usually signal that they're leaving before they actually say goodbye.

  • "Anyway, I should get going."A natural signal that the conversation is ending.
  • "It was really nice talking to you."Warm and polite.
  • "See you later / See you soon."Informal goodbyes.
  • "Take care!"Friendly and suitable for almost anyone.

Practise it: a two-minute role-play

  • Imagine meeting a neighbour in a lift or on the street.
  • Use one opener, two "keep going" phrases, and one way to end the conversation.
  • Say the whole conversation out loud, playing both people.
  • Do it again tomorrow, choosing different phrases.
